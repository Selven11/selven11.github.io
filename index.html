A walking stick that missed the stairs

A friend and I built a cane for people with low vision, using a micro:bit and ultrasonic sensors. We tested it near a staircase, and it missed the drop. Sensors pointed forward cannot see a step down.

What broke, and why

  1. Night lightFour LEDs in series off four batteries. One LED died and the whole string went dark.In series, one open LED opens the loop. The fix was groups of 2, 2 and 3 in parallel, off one 12 V battery.
  2. Walking stickForward-facing ultrasonic sensors missed the drop at a staircase.A sensor pointed forward can't see a step down.
  3. Rotating TVAt Nationals the gear chewed the boxes instead of turning over them.A part came loose.
  4. AeroWattAveraged data said the drone was six times more sensitive to heat than it was.Averaging hid where the windows didn't line up. Aligning the traces caught it.
  5. ROV boardThe first regulator I picked needs 18 V or more. The bench supplies 12 V.I switched to an LM61460 buck, which runs from 12 V.

Measuring a drone's power, one rail at a time

AeroWatt puts a current monitor on every power rail of a drone's flight computer, so you can see what each part costs in watts. I built the test bench, calibrated it against a lab instrument and ran the trials.

  • 6 railseach with an INA226 current monitor
  • 1.07%mean agreement with a Monsoon power monitor
  • 54trials, more than 48 million samples
  • 6×false thermal sensitivity from an averaging artifact, caught and corrected

Second of nine authors on the paper, accepted at IEEE MILCOM 2026.

The power board I'm designing now

The test bench runs the ROV's thrusters, solenoids and Raspberry Pi off 12 V. This board takes power from any of three inputs, protects it, steps it down and reports every rail to the ROV's CAN bus.

  1. Inputs 12 V from an XT60, a barrel jack or USB-C. The AP33772S asks a USB-C charger for 12 V. Ideal diodes pick the live source and block reverse current.
  2. Protection The LM5069 limits inrush and cuts out above 16.2 V. The SMBJ15A clamps spikes.
  3. Conversion An LM61460 makes 5 V for the Pi. The first regulator I looked at needs 18 V or more, so it can't run from 12 V.
  4. Monitoring Four INA237s and a TMP1075 report to an STM32, which sends every rail to the control station over CAN.
